SIRAI / FIRST-TIME GUIDE

A practical way to get started.

Each tool has an interactive fictional example. Load one, adjust the assumptions, and examine the results. Nothing shown here is a verified SIRAI field project.

WATERSHARE / DEMONSTRATION

Plan shared water collection

Fictional example: 24 households × 35 litres per household = 840 litres daily, split across three windows.

  1. Open WaterShare and load the fictional example.
  2. Check daily demand against the 900 L assumed supply and 1,000 L tank.
  3. Change household counts and water quantities, then review warnings.
  4. Export a CSV only if it is safe to store collection times.

Do not publicly share water-point locations, movement times or household identities.

Open WaterShare and load example

PROJECTSTARTER / DEMONSTRATION

Turn a community idea into a draft

Fictional example: coordinate shared access to water using sample costs, roles, milestones, and risk responses.

  1. Open ProjectStarter and load the fictional example.
  2. Review the problem and ask who should be consulted.
  3. Replace invented costs with locally verified prices; check the funding gap.
  4. Review responsibilities, milestones and safeguarding before exporting.

An example does not establish funding, community consent or a SIRAI project.

Open ProjectStarter and load example

TAQAWISE / DEMONSTRATION

Understand a household energy scenario

Fictional example: lamps, fan, phone charging, refrigerator and television, with sample battery assumptions.

  1. Open TaqaWise and load the fictional example.
  2. Inspect daily electricity consumption and essential loads.
  3. Change backup hours, battery capacity and other assumptions to compare scenarios.
  4. Download the editable draft or print the report; consult a qualified electrician before any installation.

The estimate omits some real-world design factors. Do not use it to purchase or wire equipment.

Open TaqaWise and load example

Try a safe usability test

Ask a volunteer to use the website on their own phone. Participation must be voluntary. Do not request names, exact locations, or sensitive activity details.

  1. Ask whether they can find and load an example without assistance.
  2. Ask them to change one input, and explain what changed in the result.
  3. Check Arabic language, mobile layout, keyboard use, and offline access.
  4. Record only non-identifying problems and suggestions, with consent.
  5. Submit findings through the feedback form while connected.

Offline: open all tools once while connected, then test again with mobile data disabled. Feedback submission requires internet. Opening pages that have not cached may fail.

ProjectStarter.

Every meaningful project starts with a good question.

Take a community idea from first conversation to a clear, realistic plan. One step at a time, with the people it is meant to serve.

A PRACTICAL STARTING POINT

Ideas are everywhere. A plan makes them possible to discuss.

Whether you want to organize a learning circle, support an environmental activity, or improve a shared space, ProjectStarter helps you ask the important questions before you spend money or make promises.

THE WORKSPACE

Make your idea actionable.

Use the guided steps. You can return to any part and revise your plan.

The best projects start by listening, not assuming.

01 / 05

Start with the people and the problem.

Describe what you want to change, who it affects, and how you will know whether your effort helped.

Examples are starting points, not operational instructions. Confirm needs with local participants and do not record identifying or sensitive information. Selecting an example replaces your current draft.

Keep it short and easy to explain.
Describe a real need, not just an idea for an activity.
Choose something observable. Avoid promising impact before it happens.
Think about feedback, accessibility and decision-making.
02 / 05

Know what it takes before spending.

Add realistic estimates, keep contingency separate, and see where funding is still needed.

A buffer for uncertainty, not money already available.
Separate secured funding from ideas that are still uncertain.

03 / 05

Decide who does what.

Define responsibilities, not personal identities. Use role labels rather than names for sensitive work.

Project responsibilities

Include a point of contact and someone who can receive concerns.

Do not collect unnecessary personal or sensitive information about community members.
04 / 05

Break ambition into manageable steps.

Give each milestone a realistic target date or timeframe. Identify risks before they become emergencies.

Milestones

What happens first, next and last?

Risks and responses

Think about safety, access, costs, local trust and changing conditions.

Do not publish participant names, exact sensitive locations or details that could put people at risk.
05 / 05

Your idea, clearly presented.

Review your plan, identify what is missing, and export a draft to discuss with others.

Share only non-identifying details. Never publish water-point locations, movement times or volunteer identities.
